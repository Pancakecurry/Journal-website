# Product Principles

These principles govern product decisions. Detailed visual guidance lives in the [Campus Atelier design language](../ux/CAMPUS_ATELIER_DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md).

## 1. The Day before the data model

Organize the experience around what happened on a particular day. Content types may support the system, but they must not dominate the user's mental model.

## 2. Capture before categorization

The shortest path is plain input and save. Types, tags, trackers, and structure are optional enhancements. Messy human information is welcome.

## 3. Every day is valid

One sentence, many photographs, a long reflection, or nothing at all are legitimate outcomes. No incomplete-day status, guilt, streak pressure, or completion theater.

## 4. Quietly expressive, never performative

The product is calm, warm, polished, and slightly playful. Personality comes from the user's authorship and history rather than an excess of stickers, effects, preset identity, or decorative noise.

## 5. Concentration makes the interface quieter

Use the Desk → Notebook → Paper intensity model: expressive for inhabiting and browsing, organized for finding and managing, focused for writing and reflection.

## 6. Privacy is foundational

Personal writing and photographs require strong isolation, secure access, understandable controls, deletion, export, backups, and app locking. Do not claim end-to-end encryption or other guarantees until implemented and verified.

## 7. Core journaling is local-friendly

The product must be designed so core journaling can eventually work without internet access. The technical approach remains undecided until Task 001 and subsequent architecture work.

## 8. AI is optional and consentful

Basic journaling never depends on AI. Any future AI capability must be clearly identified, controllable, and scoped to content categories the user understands and permits.

## 9. Play supports memory

Optional play belongs in My World and must not compete with journaling. When useful, it produces authored artifacts—a doodle attached to a day, for example. It does not create currencies, pressure, or an optimization loop.

## 10. Depth over placeholders

Meaningful product slices include persistence, responsive behavior, empty/loading/failure states, offline implications, accessibility, reduced motion, privacy, testing, maintainability, and visual polish where applicable.
